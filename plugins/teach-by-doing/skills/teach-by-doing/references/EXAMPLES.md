# teach-by-doing — worked example

The canonical demonstration. Born in a live workshop ("סדנת סוכני AI · בנימין טק", meeting 2)
while teaching one principle: **your agent should be the single source of truth — not just
where your business data lives, but the one interface you keep working through.**

That principle has five small steps. Naked, it's a lecture. With `teach-by-doing` applied,
each step becomes a rep the learner does in the room:

| # | Principle / step | Rep (2–5 min, on the computer) | Rung | Mental muscle |
|---|------------------|--------------------------------|------|---------------|
| 1 | Get comfortable in the environment / "the bare office" | **Home tour:** open the agent in your folder and ask — *"Tell me what you know about my business right now, and what you're missing to help me with everything."* | 1 | Talking to the agent unpressured; seeing how bare the office is; getting a furnishing backlog |
| 2 | One gateway / single source of truth | **The redirect:** take a question you'd normally send to Google/ChatGPT — ask it *inside your folder* instead. | 1 | Routing everything to one source until it's reflex |
| 3 | Furnish the empty office | **One piece of furniture:** pick one item off the tour's backlog and add it now — a knowledge file, or one integration. | 1 | Closing a gap immediately; feeling the office fill |
| 4 | Involve the agent in *thinking*, not just doing | **Thinking partner:** bring a real dilemma (not a task) — *"I'm considering X, what should I weigh?"* — and ask it to argue the opposite too. | 1 | Consulting, not commanding |
| 5 | Feel at home | **Make it mine:** tell the agent who you are and how you want it to talk to you → it writes/updates your `AGENTS.md`. | 1 | Making the space personal and pleasant |

The arc: **tour → redirect → furnish → consult → make-it-mine.** Step 1 is the strongest rep
in the set because it does three things at once (comfort + revealing the gap + producing the
backlog the next steps act on).

## Note on rungs in this example

Every step here landed on **rung 1** because the learners had a live agent in front of them.
Had the principle been about, say, a routine that only pays off over weeks, the rep would have
dropped to **rung 2** (open the interface and scaffold the routine now) or **rung 3** (write,
in a notebook, the trigger and the steps you'll wire up later). The principle still gets a
present-tense rep — that's the rule.
