Final Verdict — 4 Experiments

Exp 1 — Passive seeds: Zero effect (d=-0.02, p=0.925). Seeds as silent context do nothing.

Exp 2 — Active seeds vs plain: Huge effect (d=1.89, p<0.001) but confounded — judge not truly blind, outputs reference seed content directly.

Exp 3 — Seeds vs "Be Creative!": "Be creative!" BEATS seeds (d=-0.77, p<0.001). Seeds lose on 4/4 tasks.

Exp 4 — Seeds+Creative vs Creative (blind pairwise): Seeds win 63.3% overall (p=0.052, borderline). Strongly task-dependent: seeds hurt brand names (27%) but dominate out-of-office emails (93%, p=0.001).

Bottom line: Creative seeds have a task-dependent effect. They help most on tasks that benefit from unexpected metaphors and narrative framing (emails, descriptions), but hurt on tasks needing focused precision (naming). For generic creative tasks, simply saying "be wildly creative" is equally effective and much simpler.

Signal worth noting: The tasks were ordered by output length — Brand Names (shortest, list) → Describe Hope (paragraph) → New Ritual (paragraph+) → Out-of-Office (full email). Seeds' win rate increases almost perfectly with output length: 27% → 60% → 73% → 93%. This suggests seeds may be most effective on longer, narrative-heavy tasks where there's more "room" for unexpected metaphors to shape the output. All 4 tasks were still relatively short — seeds might have even stronger effects on long-form creative writing.
